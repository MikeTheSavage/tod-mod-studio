TOD Mod Studio

A level editor and mod manager for Total Overdose (2005). This guide explains, in plain words, what it does and how to use it. Inside the app it opens with the ? button at the top or the F1 key.

1What it is

TOD Mod Studio lets you change Total Overdose's levels and game values without any other software. You can:

  • look around every level in 3D, with the game's real models and textures;
  • move, add and remove things in a level: pickups, weapons, enemies, vehicles, invisible walls, trigger boxes;
  • change numbers the game uses, such as weapon damage, clip sizes and car handling, and try them in a small playground first;
  • bring in your own 3D models and pictures, or replace the game's;
  • add new maps and your own missions;
  • test your mod in the game with one button, in a private copy that leaves your own game alone;
  • put your changes into the game with one click, and take them out again with one click;
  • share your mod with friends as a small file.

You need your own installed copy of Total Overdose (for example the GOG version). The app does not include any of the game's files.

2How it works

1. ReadThe app reads your installed game.
2. CacheIt copies what it needs into a cache on your PC, once.
3. EditYou change levels and values in the app.
4. BuildIt checks your changes and builds one mod file.
5. PlayThe game loads that file first, so your changes win.

The game's own files are never changed. Your mods live in one extra file, the pack, next to the game. The app adds that file to the game's list of files to load (in configpc.txt) and keeps a backup of that list. Removing your mods deletes the extra file and restores the list, and the game is exactly as it was.

Before anything is written, the app checks every change: the parts you did not touch must stay identical, nothing may point at something that does not exist, and the game's limits must be respected. If a check fails, nothing is installed and the app tells you what is wrong.

3Installing

  1. Run TODModStudio-Setup.exe. You do not need administrator rights.
  2. Keep the suggested folder or pick another one, choose your shortcuts, and click Install.
  3. Start it from the Start menu: TOD Mod Studio. This guide is there too: TOD Mod Studio Guide.
Windows may show "Windows protected your PC" because the installer is not signed by a company. Click More info, then Run anyway.

The app's window needs the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime. Windows 10 and 11 normally have it already. If Setup says it is missing, install it from Microsoft's website first.

To uninstall: Windows Settings → Apps → Installed apps → TOD Mod Studio → Uninstall. Your mods stay in your library unless you tick the box to delete them too. Remove your mods from the game first (see Versions, backups and restore to original) if you want the game back to normal.

To update: run a newer Setup. It replaces the program and keeps your mods and settings. After an update the app shows What's new once.

4The first start

  1. The welcome screen asks Where is your game installed? Pick your Total Overdose folder: the one that contains TOD.exe and blocks.naz. For GOG that is usually C:\GOG Games\Total Overdose. Press Use this folder.
  2. The One-time setup step copies the levels into the app's cache. This takes a few minutes and a few hundred MB of disk space. It only happens once. You can press Continue without waiting: the progress stays in the bar at the bottom of the window, and a level you open waits for the reading to finish.
  3. If you have the TOD HD graphics mod installed, the app notices and builds your mods on top of it.
  4. When setup is done the app offers a short walkthrough: place a crate and test it in the game. You can skip it and start it later from Help.

Finding your way around

The bar on the left lists the areas. The top bar shows the screens of the area you are in, your mod's name, how many changes are not saved yet, and the Save mod and New buttons. The bar at the bottom shows your game folder, whether the game is running, what is installed in the game, any job in progress, the Problems count, the worst limit and how many of the 17 new-mission slots are free.

The app starts in Advanced mode, which shows everything. Beginner mode hides the screens that most people rarely need (Routes, Sections, Collision, Mission logic and Find a bad mod) and folds away the Advanced panels. Nothing is deleted: switch back at any time in Settings or in the Help window, and links to those screens keep working.

5Help, lessons and examples

The ? button in the top bar (or F1) opens the Help window. It opens at the part of this guide that matches the screen you are on. The working screens have no teaching text of their own: hover over a control for a short tooltip, and open Help for the rest.

  • Guide. This guide, with a search box. Type a word and the matching sections are listed; click one to jump to it.
  • Lessons. Short step-by-step lessons that run inside the real editor. A small note points at the next control and checks that you did the step. Next goes on, Skip ends the lesson. Lessons: Place a crate and test it in the game, Change a weapon, Add a mission.
  • Examples. Three small finished projects to open and look at: a level tweak, a tuning tweak and a race mission. They contain only your kind of changes, never the game's files.
  • Glossary. What words such as section, loader, trigger, route, archive, pack, block and giver mean.
  • What's new. What changed in the last updates.

6Editing a level

Open a level

Go to World and the Level editor screen. In the bar at the top pick a Map, then a Section of that map, or Whole map. The level opens as soon as you choose. If you also want a mission's enemies, pickups and vehicles, open the Missions menu, tick one or more missions and press Open with these.

Change things

  1. Click an object in the 3D view or in the list on the left (the Objects button at the top shows or hides that list; the chips and the search box narrow it down).
  2. Drag the arrows to move it. The Select, Move, Rotate and Resize buttons at the top switch the tool: G moves, R rotates, T resizes boxes.
  3. The panel on the right shows its position, its type (which pickup, weapon or vehicle) and more. Type new values there if you want exact numbers. Advanced holds the collision flags and every property.
  4. Add puts a new object in the level: a pickup, weapon, enemy, vehicle, trigger box, invisible wall, navigation blocker or route point. Choose the kind, then click the ground to place it; R turns it before you click.
  5. Duplicate (Ctrl+D) copies the selected object. Delete removes it. Both are at the bottom of the right panel.
  6. Made a mistake? Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y redoes. Every editing action can be undone.

The Show menu hides or shows kinds of objects, the level's models and its collision (the invisible shapes you bump into, coloured by type; every colour also has a name in the legend). The Edit list chip in the left panel lists every change you made so far.

Check tests your changes without installing anything and lists any problems, with a count of what needs a look, for example too many objects in a section or too many drivable cars in one place (the game allows 4 at once).

Some sections cannot take new objects (the app tells you which). New walls for those go into the map's shared layer instead, which is loaded with every section of that map.

7The 3D view

One 3D view draws every map in the app: the Level editor, New maps, Mission templates, Sections, Test saves and Mission logic all use it, so it behaves the same everywhere.

  • 3D and Top. The switch in the view's corner changes between a free camera and a view straight down with north at the top. Top view pans with W A S D or a left drag and zooms toward the mouse.
  • Picking and placing. Click to pick. While you place something, a see-through copy follows the mouse; click to put it down. In screens that ask for a position and a facing, press, drag and release to set both.
  • Overlays. Boxes, rings, paths and labels the screens draw over the map (loaders, spots, zones, routes, mission triggers). A menu can hide each layer.
  • Snapping. Grid, angle and height: Surface follows what is under the mouse, Ground goes down through roofs to the floor, Free does not snap.
  • Controller and keyboard. Everything the mouse does here can also be done from a controller or the keyboard (see Controller support).

Heavy maps stream in while you look around, and a progress box shows how many models have arrived. The Map button in the screens that only use the map for context turns the models off to make it faster.

8Editor tools

  • Selecting many. Ctrl or Shift click adds to the selection. Drag a box in Select mode (or hold Shift and drag). Ctrl+A selects all, Shift+A selects everything of the same model. In the list, Shift picks a range and Ctrl toggles one.
  • Group moves. Move or turn the selection together, change one property on many objects at once, move by an exact amount, align and spread them out, or drop them to the ground.
  • Snapping. Grid and angle steps for moves and turns, and a height mode (see the 3D view).
  • Prefabs and copy buffer. Save a group of objects as a prefab and stamp it anywhere, also in another map or mod. Prefabs, shortcuts and the copy buffer belong to you, not to one mod.
  • Scatter brush. Paint pickups, weapons, enemies or vehicles over an area with a radius, density, spacing and random turn. One stroke is one undo step.
  • Outliner. Search, filter chips, hide, lock and isolate (Ctrl+I); Alt+H shows all again. Layers are named groups you can hide or lock; they are saved with the mod.
  • Ruler and scale figure. Measure distances in metres, or put a 1.8 m figure next to something to judge its size.
  • Camera spots and notes. Save a viewpoint, or pin a note in the level, and come back to it later.
  • Find and replace. Ctrl+H swaps one kind of pickup, weapon or vehicle for another everywhere in the mod.
  • Command search. Ctrl+K lists every command of the open screen and a way to go to any screen. Keyboard shortcuts (in the Tools menu or the command search) lets you rebind keys. W A S D Q E stay reserved for flying.

9Changing game values

The Game data screen (area Tune) changes numbers the game keeps in its data files. Pick the group at the top, use Find to search, and switch on Only changed to see just what you edited. A table row is one weapon or vehicle: click a cell to type a new number, or pick a row to use the sliders on the right. A changed value shows its old number crossed out, and Use the default puts it back. The groups:

  • Weapons: damage, clip size, fire rate, range, ammo;
  • Vehicles: handling, health, cameras, traffic;
  • Moves and slow motion: power-up limits, stamina costs, combo rewards;
  • Camera: where the camera sits behind you and in cars, and how far you can see.

Your changes belong to the mod you are working on. A few things (such as field of view) are built into the game's program itself; the app lists those but cannot change them.

10Tune previews and the Playground

Each group in Game data has a live picture of what your numbers do, so you can judge a change before you put it in the game:

  • Weapons: the weapon model, rings that show the damage at each distance, the spread cone and tracers at the fire rate.
  • Vehicles: the car on a moving road with a speed gauge and its turning circle.
  • Moves: Ram playing the move, with a timeline of the slow-motion speed.
  • Camera: the camera's view of Ram, and a small picture seen through it.

The Playground button lets you actually try the numbers: a shooting range for the weapon you picked, a driving test for the car, and a walking test with slow motion. It uses your changed values and the controller or the keyboard. It is a rough model of the game, close enough to judge a change but not the real game; use Test in game for that.

11Bringing in a model

Open Create and Import model. Pick a 3D file of your own (.glb, .gltf or .obj) and choose what it becomes:

  • A new model you can then place in a level from the Level editor's Add list; or
  • A replacement for one of the game's models: pick the game model, and every copy of it in the game changes.

Set the direction (which way is up), the scale (the screen can size it to match the original or to the height of Ram) and where the model's origin sits. The app reduces the model to what the game can load, bakes its pictures, and builds a simple collision shape (hull, box or pieces) if you want one. The Limits panel on the screen shows how close you are to the game's limits for models and pictures. Characters cannot be replaced, and a model that TOD HD or another mod already replaced is marked: yours goes on top of it.

For a whole city or district, use the Large scene button at the top right of this screen (see Bringing in a whole district from your own 3D file).

12Textures

Open Create and Textures. Every picture in the game is listed once, with the models that use it. Pick one, load your own picture (PNG, JPEG, BMP, TGA or DDS) and see it on the model. The game has strict rules about picture shapes, so the screen offers sizes of the original's shape, can Crop to power of two, keeps see-through pictures see-through, and lists its Checks. Revert puts the original picture back.

13The catalogue

The Assets area (bottom of the left bar) holds the Catalogue: every model in your game as a card with a picture.

  • Narrow the list by category, map, size and triangle count, or by used models; search by name and sort.
  • Pick a card to see a live 3D preview. Switch on the wireframe, the collision shape, the bones or a 1.8 m figure.
  • For models with a skeleton, pick an animation clip and play it.
  • The detail panel lists measures, where the model is used (map, section, mission) and its textures. Clicking a place opens the Level editor there, clicking a texture opens Textures.
  • Place in level starts placing it in the Level editor. Replace opens Import model with the target chosen.

The first time, the app reads the whole game to build the list; after that it opens at once.

14Putting your mod in the game

  1. Click Save mod at the top to keep it in your library.
  2. Go to Play and the Mods and install screen. Switch on the mods you want. When two mods change the same value, the one lower in the list wins; drag a mod to reorder the list. The Profile menu keeps named lists of switched-on mods, and Community next to Library lists mods from the community list set in Settings.
  3. Close the game, then click Install enabled mods into the game in the bar at the bottom. The panel on the right, In the game now, shows what the game has: mods, new maps, new missions.
  4. Start Total Overdose and play.

To go back to the normal game: close the game and click Remove all mods. The app asks first and tells you what is kept.

15Test in game

You do not have to install a mod to try it. Test in game (in the Level editor bar, the mod library and the Debug area) does everything in one go:

  1. Check your mod (it stops here if something is wrong);
  2. Build it into a private copy of the game. The copy shares the big files with your game and uses little extra disk space; your own game folder is not touched;
  3. Test copy: your enabled mods, new maps and missions and the open mod go into the copy only;
  4. Test save: a save that starts you where you want (a chosen spot, full health, weapons, and so on);
  5. Launch the game.

The small bars next to the button show which step is running. While the game runs the button becomes Stop. If the game closes with an error, a Crashed button opens a short report of what it was loading. The arrow next to the button has Test settings (where to start and what to give the player) and Test in my own game, which does install the mod into your real game after asking, and puts your saves back afterwards. If your drives differ the app tells you how much a full copy costs and asks first. The Settings screen can keep the private copy after the game closes.

16Debug

The Debug area is for when something does not work.

  • Test run: the options for Test in game, the state of the last run, the blocks the game loaded, the slowest loads and the memory it used, and the game's own log.
  • Find a bad mod: if the game crashes and you do not know which mod does it, the app turns half of your mods on, asks whether the problem still happens, and halves again until it finds the one (or tells you that it needs two together).
  • Support: a Support bundle is a small file for someone who helps you. It holds your version, game and mod names and the last test's log. Your user name and folders are replaced before it is made.

17Limits and Problems

The game has hard limits (how many objects a section holds, how big a model or picture may be, how many archives it can load, and so on). The app measures how much of each limit your mods use.

  • The meter strip at the top of the main screens shows the limits that matter for that screen. Each meter shows a number, a bar and a symbol: a tick for fine, a triangle for getting close (70 % or more, with a striped bar), a cross for over the limit (a cross-hatched bar), a dash when the app cannot tell. Tooltips and screen readers give the state in words. Colour alone never carries the meaning.
  • Limits (Play area) lists every limit with its value, scope, source and how sure the app is of it.
  • Problems (the status bar and the drawer that opens from it) lists everything the app found: live rules, the last Check and any meter that is amber or red. Show me jumps to the thing that is wrong.

Some limits would crash the game when passed. For those the app refuses to install or export and there is no override. The others are warnings.

18Versions, backups and restore to original

  • Autosave. Your unsaved changes are kept a few seconds after each change. If the app closes, the next start brings them back.
  • Versions (button in the top bar): name a point in your mod's life and go back to it later. Keeps up to 30 per mod.
  • Backups. Before the app changes the game's files or its list of archives it makes a backup. Mods and install lists them in Game files, where you can restore or delete one. Verify checks the game files and can bring back one that changed.
  • Restore to original. The button in Game files removes everything Mod Studio put in the game and saves a backup first. Your mod library, projects and saves are kept.
  • Game changed since install. If TOD HD or Gameplay+ changes after you installed, the status bar says so; Rebuild makes the pack again on top of it.

19Sharing mods

  • Share (top right), then The selected mod (click a mod in the list first, or use the … menu on its row), saves your mod as a .todmod file. The switched-on mods as a collection and One piece (a prefab, routes, tuning or a mission) share more or less than one mod. It is small because it is a list of your changes (a recipe), not a copy of the game's files. Models and pictures you added travel inside it.
  • Your friend uses Import… on the same screen in their TOD Mod Studio. Their copy builds the mod from their own game when they install it.
  • Mods shared by older versions of TOD Mod Studio also import here.

21Your saved games and save copies

Total Overdose saves remember objects by the order they were created. To keep your old saves working, the app:

  • adds new objects after the existing ones, never in between;
  • by default does not truly delete objects. It "retires" them: they move far below the world with their collision off, so you never see them. You can change this in Settings, but truly deleting can break saves made in that section, and the checker will warn you.

Test saves

In Test saves you can Start at a chosen spot (the map of the save is shown; click the place, or use Place to set position and facing) or Start from one of your saves.

Save copies

Before the app installs or removes anything it copies your whole save folder (the last 20 copies are kept). Save copies on the same screen lists them, and Restore puts one back. Update a save for new missions makes an updated copy of a save that knows your new missions; it never changes the original save.

Open Create and New maps. A map project holds one new map and how players get into it. It is separate from your ordinary mod; its files are kept in the newmaps folder beside your saved mods and settings. The Map project menu at the top switches between projects. The five areas next to it are the steps, left to right:

Start a project

  1. Press New map project. Pick a start: Blank, Desert compound, Canyon road, Town block or Shipped map copy (a copy of one of the game's maps). For the first four, pick a size, Small, Medium or Large; the tiles show the side in metres and how much of the game's limits it uses.
  2. Choose where the first entrance goes (Entrance in), give the Package name and press Create. To open a recipe you already have, paste its path under Import a recipe and press Import.
  3. The map's name must have exactly as many letters as the game map it is built from. The Map area shows the count (for example 18/18) next to the name, and a button suggests a free name.

Atlas

One top view shows every shipped map and your new maps. Click Place on the layout, then click where the map goes; R turns it. Placement on the right has three choices:

  • Separate (the usual one): the map has its own space. The picture is only an editor layout, so the game does not care where you put it, but a warning appears when a map overlaps another one or sits outside the safe box.
  • Extension: the map is a section inside one of the game's maps. Choose the Host section, then draw the region it covers on the host and choose what is carried over.
  • Aligned: the map sits next to a neighbour. Pick the edge (north, east, south or west), press Snap to the neighbour and read the Seam line, which tells you if the ground at the join differs.

Entrances

An entrance is an invisible box in one of the game's maps. Walking into it (or pressing the use key in it) loads your map. The list on the left holds your entrances; Add an entrance makes one and Table lists all of them together with the entrances of your other projects. The left view shows the game's map (Host), the right view shows your map (New map).

  1. Select the entrance and pick its In section. A section that cannot take an entrance says so.
  2. Press Place box and click the host view where the box goes; press and drag to set its facing. Size, height and facing can also be typed under Trigger box. Fires on chooses Touch or Use.
  3. Pick the Icon (Church, Bullring, Downtown, Harbour, Industrial, Border or Angel) and the Beam (None, Slim or Full) the player sees at the box.
  4. Press Place arrival and click the new map's view, dragging to turn the player the way you want. This is where the player lands. Check that there is ground under it. If you made named arrivals in the Map area, choose one in Arrives at; otherwise the entrance lands at the Main arrival.
  5. Set the Way back: a box in your map (Place way back) and the spot in the game's map where the player lands again. Switch on One-way if there is none.
  6. In Advanced mode, Options has By vehicle (the box is for cars, such as a tunnel; the car arrives with the player) and Locked until mission (the entrance opens after that mission was the last one played).

The map needs at least one entrance that is not locked, so the player can always get out; the list says so under it. Checks at the bottom of the panel lists what is wrong with the selected entrance.

An entrance arrival, a named arrival or the map's main arrival must not lie inside a mission giver's box. Check refuses it, because the game can stop when the player lands there. Move one of them out of the other.

Map

The list on the left holds the Arrival spot, the Return spot (where Ram goes back to in the game's map; press it to add one) and any Named arrivals (Add a named arrival). Pick one and press Place, then click the view, or type its numbers on the right. Positions are in metres: x and z run along the ground, y is height. Map in the corner of the view draws a shipped map under your spots. The right panel also holds the Map card: name, description, author, theme, size and a picture.

The Build panel on the view has the tools. Each is a tab; every stroke is one undo step, and the meters under the tabs show how close the map is to the game's limits.

Ready and Manage

Ready says Ready to install or Install blocked and lists every error and warning per map and for the project, with the usual counts: sections in the game, maps, and the quadtree sizes. Click a row to go to the place that fixes it. Install stays blocked while an error is left.

Manage shows the numbers your project has taken, the map projects and whether each is in the game, which sections are shared, and Export writes the map as a package into a folder or file you name.

Check, Build, Install

  1. Press Save project. In the Into the game bar press Check; Details lists each PASS, WARN or FAIL message. Check reads the recipe and the destination names.
  2. Build writes the map and checks its objects, collision and the finished package. A big map takes a few minutes.
  3. Close the game and press Install into the game. The app puts the package first in the game's loading list and backs up configpc.txt. If an installed mod or another map package changes the same part of the game, remove that one first. Uninstall package (it asks first) removes just this project's package. Save and build again after you change anything.

You can also try the map without installing it: Test in game in the Play area builds a private copy.

New missions for your map are made in Mission templates (see Adding your own missions); the game takes 17 new missions in all.

23Bringing in a whole district from your own 3D file

A big 3D scene, such as a city district, can become a new map. Use a file of your own that you have the right to use. The app cuts it down to what the game can load, bakes the lighting and builds collision, so you can walk on it.

  1. Source. Open Create → Import model and press Large scene (Single model goes back). Paste the path of a .glb or .gltf file or press Choose…. The page shows its size, parts, materials and pictures. If the scene lies on its side or upside down, change Direction (Y up, Z up, Upside down, the flip and the turn). Press Next.
  2. Scale. Press Measure, click both ends of something of a known size (a door about 2.1 m, a lane about 3.5 m, a car about 4.5 m) and type its real length, then Apply. The figure of Ram stands beside the scene so you can judge it, and the streets can be compared with Downtown's.
  3. Region. On the top view draw the part you want (Rectangle, Polygon or Square with a side). Clip cuts at the border; Whole parts keeps every part the shape touches. The counts show the triangles, parts, materials and pictures inside. A region of a few hundred metres across is plenty; a larger one builds slowly and may not fit the game's limits.
  4. Clean. Keep the defaults: repeated, stray and empty triangles are dropped and vertices are welded.
  5. Textures. Keep the defaults: identical pictures are merged, small ones are packed together and the set is shrunk to the budget.
  6. Sections. The scene is split into objects, and the meters above the page show each limit. Change nothing unless one turns amber or red; then Reduce triangles helps.
  7. Collision. Solid ground and walls are made from the walkable surfaces. The surface type follows each picture and can be changed per picture. A step taller than the game's player can climb is reported by Check.
  8. Send. Name it and choose New map. Pick A new project, or the project that already holds this map to send it again. Place is the x and z of the district's middle in the world: choose free ground that no shipped map covers, and look at the Atlas to see that it does not overlap one. Press Send to new map, then Open in New maps. Sending writes the map, its name, walls along the edge, the sea and a ring of far towers. Sending again with another scale or Place moves the arrival, way back, entrances, walls and missions with it.
  9. Entrances. In New maps → Entrances choose a game map such as Downtown, put the box on the path the player walks, put the arrival on a street and turn it to face down the street, and set the way back. Pick the icon and beam. For a car entrance such as a tunnel, switch on By vehicle on a second entrance whose box sits where a road goes down. Ready must show no error.
  10. Look. In the Map area open Look and pick the City day preset. Keep Bake lighting on at Normal and the Skyline on. Then Write minimap in the Minimap tab (Advanced mode).
  11. Missions. In Mission templates start a project on your map and add a kind such as Ghost Run. Put the giver a few metres from the arrival, not on top of it. The plan of the district lies under the placement view.
  12. Check, Build, Install. Press Check until it is clean; the map-area row passes only when the district stands on its own ground. Build takes several minutes for a big district. Then Install into the game, or try it first with Test in game.
A scene made for another purpose often has water, glass and trees that need a look in the game. Walk the district after the first build and send it again with changes if something is wrong.

24Adding your own missions

Making a mission

Open Create and Mission templates. A mission project holds one or more missions that take place on a map of yours (a project from New maps, including a district brought in from a 3D file) or in a part of an existing map such as Downtown.

  1. Click New project, or choose Open a sample…: Mesa Compound shows all 17 kinds on one map (it uses all 17 mission slots), the yard sample is a short two-mission example, and A race in the yard is a single checkpoint race.
  2. On the left, under Maps for the missions, press + Add a map: choose one of your new maps, or a section of an existing map. Sections that cannot take a mission giver are greyed; hover to see why. For an existing section, give its walkable play area and ground height, and choose a shipped mission to start like.
  3. On the Pick a kind of mission page, click a card and press Use this kind. Then place things: the Place on the map list shows what still needs a spot; click an item and click the top view (north is up). The yellow box is the mission giver: the player steps into it and presses the use key to be offered the mission. Keep the map's arrival, its named arrivals and every entrance arrival out of this box: Check refuses a landing spot inside a giver's box, so move one of them out of the other.
  4. Change the numbers in the Settings panel on the right: a slider and a number box, with the allowed range. Use the default undoes a change, and Advanced folds away the rest.
  5. Check (in the Into the game bar, with the Check results list above it) says what to fix, in plain words (a giver on a building, a pass score nobody can earn, boxes too close together); Show me jumps to the mission. Then Build (about a minute) and, with the game closed, Install into the game.

Gameplay+

If the Gameplay+ mod is installed, the app builds the missions on top of it, so Gameplay+'s own changes stay in effect. Install Gameplay+ first, then build and install your missions.

Slots. A game can have 17 new missions in total, whatever the number of maps. The bar at the bottom of the window shows how many are left. All your installed mission projects are built together into the pack, so installing a second project rebuilds the first one with it. The game can load 8 archive files; the app refuses an install that would go over that. If a new map you use here was installed from the New maps screen, installing the missions replaces that package (the map is part of the missions package).

Old saves. A save made before you installed the missions does not know them: pressing the use key at a new mission giver would close the game. Open Play and Test saves, find Update a save for new missions, pick the save and press Make an updated copy. It never changes your save and never runs by itself; installing the copy backs up the slot it replaces. A new game, or a save made after the install, needs nothing.

Givers in an existing section (Downtown and so on). A giver box placed in a section the game already has needs one more thing in an old save: its script thread. The save update adds it when the save holds that section. A save that does not hold the section (for example one made right at the end of the game, or at the start of a mission) loads the section fresh and the box stays silent until the section is loaded again: start or finish any mission, or walk through any map loader and come back. A new game loads sections that way, so it needs nothing. Placing a goal zone on top of the giver box (the Ghost run) is refused: you accept standing in the box, and a zone only counts when you walk into it.

26Settings, accessibility and language

Everything in this section is in Settings under Appearance and Accessibility, and is remembered on this PC.

SettingWhat it does

Keyboard

Every control can be reached with the keyboard: Tab and Shift+Tab go through them, the arrow keys move between nearby controls, Enter or Space press, Esc closes a menu or a window. The control that has focus always has a clear outline. F1 opens Help and Ctrl+K opens the command search.

Screen readers

The screens that are not 3D (Mods, Game data tables, Settings, Test saves, Limits and Problems) have labels for screen readers: regions, tables, switches and progress bars say what they are. The 3D view cannot be read aloud; the same actions are in the lists beside it.

Language

To add a translation, copy the English word list to a new language file, translate the values and keep the keys, and add the language to the list of languages. The notes for this are in the locales folder of the source.
